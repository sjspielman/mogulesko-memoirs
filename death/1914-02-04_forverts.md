Mogulesko was not only the greatest and most beloved actor on the Yiddish stage, but one of the most gifted artists in the greater theater world aross all countries and all languages.
He was innately, naturally talented, but that is not all.
Nature gifted him with a remarkable charm - gifted with a generous hand.

When he used to appear on the stage, he would captive audiences with two powerful magnets at once: with his wonderful craft as a "painter-of-characters" and comedian, and with his enchanting personalities and sheer likeability. 
In both regards, for us there will never be another Mogulesko.

In the history of Jewish art, no matter which way you look, the name Mogulesko remains inscribed in giant gilded letters.

Ah, how hard it is to accept this new reality!
The heart weeps when you think of Mogulesko -- the extraordinarily lively, cheerful, good-natured Mogulesko -- who lives no more.
That sweet smile on his face has vanished forever; lost forever are those soft, beautiful eyes.

Mogulesko is dead!

Mogulesko was not yet 56 years old.
He was born on December 16th 1858 in Kalarash, Bessarabia.
He died of consumption.
He had been sick for the last 15 years with an illness of the throat.
He went to Vienna to recover, and when he returned he felt better and was able to perform for about three more years at which point the doctors told him he should no longer perform.

For Mogulesko, this was a gut punch. To not play theater! The theater was his life, his _neshuma_.

"I have lived for two things in my life," he told a representative from the _Forverts_ a few days ago \<some text is cut off in image\>.
"For the stage, and for my family."

Yes, the artist's _neshuma_ was split in two: one half belonged to the Yiddish theater which he himself helped to create, and the other to his wife and children.

And wondrous was the struggle that Mogulesko put up against death.
Time and time again he drove away the _melach hamoves_^[angel of death, ala 10th plague], not that long ago.

He woke his wife, who tended to him like a mother throughout his entire illness, in the middle of the night and said, "Oy, _mamele_, I feel so bad. I wish the day would come already, I hate the night..." 

He had wanted to say something else, but he starting choking in the throat, making a death rattle sound like a _goses_^[a Talmudic/halachic term for someone who is imminently dying].
This happened around 4 am.
His wife _macht a gevald_^[cried out in shock and/or screamed "gevald!" (gevolt)].
The neighbors rushed over and gathered and doctors were called.
Soon his two daughters arrived.
He no longer recognized anyone.

The doctors worked on him for nearly 10 hours, including an operation.
When he started to somewhat come to himself, the present writer visited him.
With a sma=ile, Mogulesko stretched out his hand toward me and said, "I'm still alive."
It's impossible to describe his tone.
With schmalz and triumph, it announced - I have conquered death.

Mogulesko knew that he days were numbered.
He felt it even more than he knew it, even though he was far from full health already.
His lungs and his nerves had long deteriorated.
His heart, his feet, his hands were weak and worn, and he had only his mind left -- his mind and his tongue.
But he did not want to give himself over to death.
Ah, how he fought with the _melach hamoves_.

He would stave death off with his sharp jokes...
He used to joke about death.
